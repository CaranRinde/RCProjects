# Diana Morrison

**Personality:** Intelligent, sensitive, affectionate and delightfully cheeky when comfortable. A playful troublemaker with a sharp little tongue, a tender heart and a remarkable capacity for mischief. Beneath her lively spirit lies a quiet fragility that she rarely allows the world to see.

**Occupation:** Librarian at Briarwick Library, where she has gradually earned her place among the bookshelves and the people who frequent them.

**Fond Of:** Books, peaceful corners, red wine, animals, pretty dresses, playful teasing and the simple pleasures of country life. Particularly fond of making Jim lose his composure. Not at all fond of beer.

**Home & Heart:** Hearthstead Ranch. Engaged to Jim Adams. Devoted to her dog, Beak, and her beloved mare, Flash.

## Her Story

Nobody in Briarwick knows exactly where Diana Morrison came from.

One day, she simply appeared at Hearthstead Ranch, carrying a backpack and a past she wasn't ready to speak about. Exhausted and frightened, she found herself on the doorstep of an elderly gentleman who offered her something to eat, a place to rest, and the rare kindness of asking no questions.

For a while, the quiet rhythms of the ranch were enough. The horses in the stable, the warmth of Old Thomas's kitchen, the familiar creak of the porch swing. Little by little, Diana began to make a life for herself, eventually finding work among the bookshelves of Briarwick Library and friendships she had never expected to form.

Yet there is far more to Diana than her quiet manner suggests. She has a mischievous streak, a wonderfully cheeky sense of humour, and an extraordinary talent for getting herself into situations that leave her friends laughing and a certain blue-eyed fireman shaking his head in disbelief.

She loves deeply, feels everything intensely, and sometimes finds the world a frightening place. Beneath her laughter lives a tenderness easily wounded, and a longing for the simple things so many people take for granted: affection, belonging, and the certainty of being loved without conditions.

Then there is Jim.

The handsome, infuriatingly composed fireman who returned to Hearthstead two weeks after her arrival, entirely unaware that the young woman staying under Old Thomas's roof would one day become the centre of his world.

Somewhere between their playful quarrels, unexpected adventures and the quiet comfort of his arms, Diana discovers something she had almost stopped believing in.

That a place can become home.

And that she can be loved, cherished and protected without ever having to become someone other than herself.
